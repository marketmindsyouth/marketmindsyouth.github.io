MEETING GUIDE 1

CAPITAL ALLOCATION

Estimated Time: 35 Minutes

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"What is the smartest $50 you have ever spent, and what is the dumbest?"

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

CHOOSE YOUR ALLOCATION: $200 CASH

OPTION A

CONSUMPTION

Buy a limited-edition jacket or a pair of sneakers today.

Value in 1 Year: $50 (Worn Out)
OPTION B

MICRO-CAPITAL

Buy a domain name, basic software, or inventory to launch a service.

Value in 1 Year: $500 to $2,000+
Discussion Question

At what exact point does spending money stop being an expense and start becoming an investment in yourself?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

Click on a guiding word below to reveal the definition for the room.

DEFINITION

Small amounts of money, typically between $50 and $300, used to acquire assets or business tools rather than standard consumer goods.

DEFINITION

A decision where the potential downside is small and firmly capped, but the potential upside is massive and open-ended.

DEFINITION

Consumption takes money out of your pocket forever. Assets actively put money back into your pocket or build concrete future value.

PHASE 4: ROOM CHALLENGE

THE $100 MICRO-BUSINESS PITCH

Form teams of 3 to 4. You have 8 minutes to plan a local business or service that requires under $100 to start. Once the timer ends, each team will take turns presenting their pitch to the room.

Example Idea

Purchasing a $60 used lawnmower and a $10 box of flyers to start a neighborhood landscaping service.

YOUR PITCH MUST ANSWER:

  • What exact tool, software, or asset did you buy with your $100?
  • Who is the very first target customer you will approach?
  • What is your specific plan to hit your first $200 in revenue?
08:00

THE BIG TAKEAWAY

Money is not just something you spend to buy things. It is a tool you allocate to create freedom and leverage.

PHASE 5: COMMUNITY CONNECTION

POST-MEETING HANGOUT

Stick around for 10 minutes! Turn to someone near you and ask them what specific micro-project or skill they want to test this year. Building wealth is a team sport.

MEETING GUIDE 2

TIME CURRENCY

Estimated Time: 35 Minutes

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"If you could instantly buy back 5 hours of your week, which specific hours would you eliminate?"

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

THE TIME TRADE-OFF MATRIX

PATH A

IMMEDIATE CASH

Work 15 hours per week at a minimum wage job ($15/hr = $225/week).

Long-Term Impact: More spending money right now.
PATH B

SKILL COMPOUNDING

Work 5 hours per week, and spend 10 hours mastering a high-income skill.

Long-Term Impact: Earning premium rates in 12 months.
Discussion Question

When is trading your time for immediate minimum wage cash a trap, and when is it genuinely necessary?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

Click on a guiding word below to reveal the definition for the room.

DEFINITION

The real cost of any choice is the value of the next best alternative you gave up.

DEFINITION

Using your youth and low financial overhead to learn high-value skills before you have adult bills to pay.

DEFINITION

Skills learned at age 16 or 17 pay professional dividends for decades. Time invested now is worth more than time invested later.

PHASE 4: ROOM CHALLENGE

THE TIME AUDIT

Form pairs. You have 8 minutes to interview each other about your schedules last week. You will present a 2-slide summary for your partner to the room.

YOUR PITCH MUST ANSWER:

  • Identify the 3 biggest "Time Leaks" in your partner's schedule.
  • If they reclaimed 4 hours next week, what specific skill or project should they spend it on?
08:00

THE BIG TAKEAWAY

You are richer in time right now than you will ever be again. Treat your hours with the same respect as cash.

PHASE 5: COMMUNITY CONNECTION

POST-MEETING HANGOUT

Don't rush out just yet. Grab a snack and chat with someone about the biggest "time leak" you discovered today. Sometimes saying it out loud makes it easier to fix.

MEETING GUIDE 3

MARKETING DEFENSE

Estimated Time: 35 Minutes

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"What is one item you bought purely because you saw it go viral on social media?"

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

THE $15 SHIRT VS. THE $250 LOGO

ITEM A

$15 PLAIN TEE

  • Material: 100% Cotton
  • Utility: Keeps you clothed and comfortable.
ITEM B

$250 DESIGNER TEE

  • Material: 100% Cotton
  • Utility: Signals status and group membership.
Discussion Question

Are you actually buying a higher quality product, or are you buying admission into a social group?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

Click on a guiding word below to reveal the definition for the room.

DEFINITION

Digital design tricks engineered to make you spend money without thinking (1-click buying, Apple Pay, countdown timers, fake scarcity).

DEFINITION

Spending money to appear wealthy to others, which actively prevents you from accumulating real wealth in the background.

DEFINITION

Understanding that free apps make money by selling your attention to advertisers. If you aren't paying for the product, you are the product.

PHASE 4: ROOM CHALLENGE

THE VIRAL BRAND TEARDOWN

Form teams of 3. You have 8 minutes to pick a viral brand (Stanley, Gymshark, Labubu, etc.) and deconstruct their marketing playbook to present to the room.

YOUR PITCH MUST ANSWER:

  • What insecurity or status goal are they targeting in young people?
  • What artificial scarcity trick do they use (drops, limited stock)?
  • How can a smart consumer enjoy this product without falling for the hype?
08:00

THE BIG TAKEAWAY

Financial literacy is mental defense. Do not let corporate algorithms dictate your bank balance.

PHASE 5: COMMUNITY CONNECTION

POST-MEETING HANGOUT

Hang out for a few minutes. Check in with the people at your table. What is one hype brand you all agree is actually worth the money, and one that is a total trap?

MEETING GUIDE 4

GEN Z JOB MARKET

Estimated Time: 35 Minutes

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"What is a modern job or career that didn't exist 10 years ago that you think is genuinely cool?"

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

THE CORPORATE PIPELINE VS. THE PORTFOLIO CAREER

THE OLD PLAYBOOK

CORPORATE LADDER

Get a degree, join one single corporate company, and climb the ladder for 40 years.

Risk Profile: Dependent on one employer.
THE MODERN PLAYBOOK

SKILL PORTFOLIO

Combine specialized skills, freelancing, multiple income streams, and digital assets.

Risk Profile: High security through diversification.
Discussion Question

Is true job security found in relying on a single employer, or in having skills that multiple people will gladly pay you for?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

Click on a guiding word below to reveal the definition for the room.

DEFINITION

Combining two or three average skills to create a unique, highly valuable combination that is hard to replace.

DEFINITION

Skills that directly generate revenue or solve major business problems, such as sales, copywriting, data analysis, or coding. They do not necessarily require a 4-year degree.

DEFINITION

Code, content, or media that works for you 24 hours a day without requiring extra labor cost on your part.

PHASE 4: ROOM CHALLENGE

THE SKILL STACK BUILDER

Pair up. You have 8 minutes to look at your partner's current interests and help them design a 2-skill stack pitch to present to the room.

YOUR PITCH MUST ANSWER:

  • What is their core passion? (Fashion, Sports, Gaming, Photography, etc.)
  • What technical or business skill could they learn to pair with it? (Data, Video Editing, Sales, etc.)
  • What is the specific micro-service they could offer local businesses this month combining the two?
08:00

THE BIG TAKEAWAY

Your specialized skills and peer network are your ultimate financial safety net.

PHASE 5: COMMUNITY CONNECTION

POST-MEETING HANGOUT

Stick around! Network with your chapter. Who in the room has a skill that you want to learn? Go ask them how they started. This is how real leverage is built.

COLLEGE ROI

Estimated Time: 35 Minutes

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"Name a college major that costs $200,000 but only pays $40,000, and one that costs $40,000 but pays $100,000."

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

THE DREAM SCHOOL TRAP

SCENARIO A

THE PRESTIGE

Go to your "Dream School" out of state. You graduate with a prestigious name but $150,000 in student loan debt at 7% interest.

Financial Reality: Paying $1,700/month for 10 years.
SCENARIO B

THE PRAGMATIST

Go to a solid in-state public university. You graduate with $0 in debt and can immediately start investing your income.

Financial Reality: Total freedom on graduation day.
Discussion Question

Is a "dream school" name on a piece of paper worth delaying buying a house, traveling, or investing for a full decade?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

Click on a guiding word below to reveal the definition for the room.

DEFINITION

Calculating how much money your degree will actually generate for you compared to exactly how much it cost you to get it.

DEFINITION

The process of paying off a loan over time. Because of high interest rates, a $100k loan often means you actually pay the bank $140k+ over 10 years.

MEETING GUIDE 6

THE ATHLETE'S WALLET

Estimated Time: 35 Minutes

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"If a local brand handed you a $50,000 NIL check today, what is the exact first thing you would buy?"

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

THE WINDFALL TRAP

SCENARIO A

THE FLASHY CAR

You take the $50k and buy a luxury SUV to look good on campus and on Instagram.

Value in 3 Years: $25,000 (Depreciating)
SCENARIO B

THE INDEX FUND

You keep driving your old car and put the entire $50k into a low-cost S&P 500 Index Fund.

Value in 10 Years: $100,000+ (Appreciating)
Discussion Question

Why do so many high-earning athletes end up bankrupt shortly after retiring?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

Click on a guiding word below to reveal the definition for the room.

DEFINITION

Assets that rapidly lose value over time (cars, clothes, jewelry). Wealth is built by avoiding depreciating assets whenever possible.

DEFINITION

When you get paid through NIL or brand deals, taxes aren't taken out automatically. If you spend the whole check, you will owe the IRS thousands at the end of the year and have no way to pay it.

MEETING GUIDE 7

STARTUP BLUEPRINT

Estimated Time: 35 Minutes

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"What is a local business that seems like a goldmine, but is probably a logistical nightmare behind the scenes?"

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

THE IDEA GUY VS. THE OPERATOR

SCENARIO A

THE "IDEA" FOUNDER

Spends 6 months designing the perfect logo, filing patents, and pitching a revolutionary app that isn't built yet.

Result: $0 in Revenue.
SCENARIO B

THE OPERATOR

Throws up a basic website in 2 hours, knocks on doors, and pre-sells a service using a basic LLC structure.

Result: Cash flow positive in week 1.
Discussion Question

Why do we romanticize "inventing things" when true wealth is usually built by building boring, scalable systems?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

Click on a guiding word below to reveal the definition for the room.

DEFINITION

A legal structure that protects your personal bank account. If your business gets sued, they can only take the business's money, not your personal savings.

DEFINITION

A business can be "profitable" on paper but still go bankrupt if the money is tied up in inventory and they don't have the "cash flow" to pay rent today.

MEETING GUIDE 8

ADULTING LOGISTICS

Estimated Time: 35 Minutes

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"What is one 'adult' financial thing—like taxes or insurance—that you are genuinely terrified of doing wrong?"

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

THE CREDIT CARD ILLUSION

SCENARIO A

THE MINIMUM PAYMENT

You max out a $2,000 card for a vacation and only pay the "minimum payment" of $35 a month.

The Math: Takes 11 years to pay off. Costs $3,800.
SCENARIO B

THE SYSTEM HACKER

You put your normal $200 groceries on the card, pay it off in full on the 28th of every month.

The Math: 0% interest paid. Perfect credit score.
Discussion Question

Credit cards are designed to trap the financially illiterate. How do you flip the system to make it work for you?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

Click on a guiding word below to reveal the definition for the room.

DEFINITION

The percentage of your credit limit you actually use. Using more than 30% of your limit tanks your credit score, even if you pay it off on time.

DEFINITION

"If I get a raise, it will push me into a higher tax bracket and I'll lose money!" FALSE. You are only taxed the higher rate on the dollars *above* the bracket line.
