MEETING GUIDE 1

CAPITAL ALLOCATION

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"What is the smartest $50 you have ever spent, and what is the dumbest?"

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

CHOOSE YOUR ALLOCATION: $200 CASH

OPTION A

CONSUMPTION

Buy a limited-edition jacket or a pair of sneakers today.

Value in 1 Year: $50 (Worn Out)
OPTION B

MICRO-CAPITAL

Buy a domain name, basic software, or inventory to launch a local service.

Value in 1 Year: $500 to $2,000+
Discussion Question

At what exact point does spending money stop being an expense and start becoming an investment in yourself?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

DEFINITION

Small amounts of money, typically between $50 and $300, used to acquire assets or business tools rather than standard consumer goods.

DEFINITION

A decision where the potential downside is small and firmly capped, but the potential upside is massive and open-ended.

DEFINITION

Consumption takes money out of your pocket forever. Assets actively put money back into your pocket or build concrete future value.

PHASE 4: ROOM CHALLENGE

THE $100 MICRO-BUSINESS PITCH

Form teams of 3 to 4. You have 8 minutes to plan a local business or service that requires under $100 to start. Each team will create a short slide deck to present to the room.

08:00

THE BIG TAKEAWAY

Money is not just something you spend to buy things. It is a tool you allocate to create freedom and leverage.

MEETING GUIDE 2

TIME CURRENCY

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"If you could instantly buy back 5 hours of your week, which specific hours would you eliminate?"

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

THE TIME TRADE-OFF MATRIX

PATH A

IMMEDIATE CASH

Work 15 hours per week at a minimum wage job ($15/hr).

Short-Term Result: More spending money today
PATH B

SKILL COMPOUNDING

Work 5 hours per week, and spend 10 hours per week mastering a high-income skill (coding, media, design).

Long-Term Result: Earning $50+/hr next year
Discussion Question

When is trading your time for immediate minimum wage cash a trap, and when is it necessary?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

DEFINITION

The real cost of any choice is the value of the next best alternative you gave up. If you spend 3 hours scrolling, the cost is not $0; it is what you could have built in those 3 hours.

DEFINITION

Taking advantage of the fact that, as a student, you have low financial overhead. This is the single easiest time in your life to spend 6 months learning a complex skill for free.

DEFINITION

Skills learned early in life pay dividends for decades. Knowledge compounds just like money in a bank account.

PHASE 4: ROOM CHALLENGE

THE TIME AUDIT

Form pairs. You have 8 minutes to review each other's weekly schedules, identify 3 "Time Leaks," and reallocate 4 hours toward a new skill or project. Present your findings to the room.

08:00

THE BIG TAKEAWAY

You are richer in time right now than you will ever be again. Treat your hours with the same respect as cash.

MEETING GUIDE 3

MARKETING DEFENSE

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"What is one item you bought purely because you saw it go viral on social media?"

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

THE $15 SHIRT VS. THE $250 LOGO

ITEM A

$15 PLAIN TEE

Material: 100% Cotton Utility: Keeps you clothed
ITEM B

$250 DESIGNER TEE

Material: 100% Cotton Utility: Signals status
Discussion Question

Are you actually buying a higher quality product, or are you buying admission into a social group?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

DEFINITION

Digital design tricks engineered to make you spend money without thinking, such as 1-click buying, countdown timers, and fake scarcity.

DEFINITION

Spending money to appear wealthy to others, which actively prevents you from accumulating real, long-term wealth.

DEFINITION

Understanding that "free" apps make money by selling your attention to advertisers. If you aren't paying for the product, you are the product.

PHASE 4: ROOM CHALLENGE

THE VIRAL BRAND TEARDOWN

Form teams of 3 to 4. You have 8 minutes to deconstruct the marketing playbook of a popular viral brand (e.g., Stanley, Supreme, Gymshark). Prepare slides to present your teardown to the room.

08:00

THE BIG TAKEAWAY

Financial literacy is mental defense. Do not let corporate algorithms dictate your bank balance.

MEETING GUIDE 4

THE GEN Z JOB MARKET

PHASE 1: THE WARM-UP

Icebreaker Question

"What is a modern job or career that did not exist 10 years ago that you think is genuinely cool?"

PHASE 2: REAL-WORLD DILEMMA

TRADITIONAL VS. PORTFOLIO CAREER

PATH A

THE OLD PLAYBOOK

Degree ↓ 1 Corporate Company ↓ 40-Year Ladder
PATH B

THE MODERN PLAYBOOK

Specialized Skills + Multiple Income Streams + Digital Assets
Discussion Question

Is true job security found in relying on a single employer, or in having skills that multiple people will pay you for?

PHASE 3: CORE CONCEPTS

DEFINITION

Combining two or three average skills to create a unique, highly valuable combination. (e.g., Video Editing + Basic Finance = High-paid media producer for financial firms).

DEFINITION

Skills that directly generate revenue or solve major business problems, such as sales, copywriting, data analysis, or coding. These often do not require a 4-year degree to start earning.

DEFINITION

Code, content, or media that works for you 24/7 without extra labor cost. Building things once that can be sold or viewed infinitely.

PHASE 4: ROOM CHALLENGE

THE SKILL STACK BUILDER

Form pairs. You have 8 minutes to design a custom "Skill Stack" for your partner based on their actual hobbies and interests. Prepare to pitch their new portfolio career to the room.

08:00

THE BIG TAKEAWAY

Your specialized skills and peer network are your ultimate financial safety net.
